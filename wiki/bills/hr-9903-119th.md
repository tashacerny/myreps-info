Specifically, it requires that prices charged for items sold through prison commissaries—retail operations where incarcerated individuals can purchase goods like food, hygiene products, and other supplies—cannot exceed fair market value. The bill aims to ensure that prices for these goods are comparable to what the same items would cost in the general marketplace.  The bill primarily affects incarcerated individuals in BOP facilities and their families, who often purchase commissary items. It also impacts the BOP's commissary operations and vendors who supply goods to federal prisons. By capping prices at fair market value, the legislation seeks to reduce the cost burden on incarcerated people and their families, who currently may pay above-market prices for commissary goods.
