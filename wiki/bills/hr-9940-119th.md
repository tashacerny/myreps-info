Specifically, the increased federal contribution would apply to two types of services for individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities: services provided in intermediate care facilities (ICFs) and home and community-based services (HCBS). The enhanced federal matching rate would be temporary and would only cover expenditures directly tied to regulatory compliance costs.  The bill affects states that administer Medicaid programs, as well as providers and individuals receiving these disability services. By increasing federal funding for compliance-related costs, the legislation aims to ease the financial burden on states when they implement or maintain federal regulatory requirements for disability services. This could indirectly affect the availability and structure of these services in participating states.
