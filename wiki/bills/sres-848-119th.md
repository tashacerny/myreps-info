The CDC, established in 1946, is a federal agency under the Department of Health and Human Services that conducts disease surveillance, research, and prevention efforts. The resolution recognizes the agency's role as a leading public health institution in the United States.  The resolution affects no individuals directly and creates no new programs, funding, or requirements. Instead, it serves a ceremonial purpose by expressing Congress's recognition of the CDC's history and contributions to public health. It would apply to the CDC as an institution and to the broader public health community.  The key provision is the formal celebration of the CDC's 80-year history.
