summary: "# Bill Summary: Congressional Approval of National Emergency Declarations  This bill would require Congress to approve national emergency declarations made by the President. Under current law, the President can declare a national emergency unilaterally under the National Emergencies Act of 1976, which grants the executive branch certain powers to respond to crises. This bill would modify that process by adding a congressional approval requirement, meaning that after the President declares an emergency, Congress would need to vote to either approve or reject the declaration.  The bill would affect the President's executive authority during emergencies, as well as Congress's role in checking that power. It would apply to any national emergency declaration made under the National Emergencies Act. Key provisions would likely include establishing a timeframe for Congress to vote on the declaration and determining what happens if Congress disapproves (such as terminating the emergency powers associated with that declaration).  The practical impact would be to shift emergency-response authority from the executive branch toward a shared approval process with the legislative branch. This would require Congress to take affirmative action to sustain emergency declarations rather than the current system, where declarations take effect unless Congress acts to block them."
