summary: "# Legislative Summary  This bill would modify the Clean Air Act by requiring federal concurrence before states can adopt and enforce certain emissions standards. Under current law, states have authority to set their own air quality standards that are more stringent than federal standards, provided they meet specific procedural requirements. This bill would add a requirement that states obtain approval or agreement from the federal government before implementing such standards.  The bill primarily affects states seeking to establish emissions regulations, air quality regulators, industries subject to emissions controls, and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA). States with existing stricter standards—such as California, which has long held a special role in setting vehicle emissions rules—and states that have followed their lead would be impacted by any new federal concurrence requirements.  Key provisions would likely include defining which emissions standards require federal concurrence, establishing a timeline and process for federal review, and specifying criteria the EPA would use to grant or deny concurrence. The exact scope—whether it applies to vehicle emissions, industrial facilities, or all state standards—would depend on the bill's specific language. The bill's title reference to \"certain State emissions standards\" suggests it targets specific categories rather than all state air quality regulations."
