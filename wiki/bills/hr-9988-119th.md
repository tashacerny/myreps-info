summary: "# Bill Summary  This bill would amend the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act to designate barley, rye, and oats as major food allergens. Currently, the law recognizes nine major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, and sesame. Adding these three grains would expand the list of allergens that food manufacturers must disclose on product labels and handle according to specific regulatory requirements.  The bill would affect food manufacturers, distributors, and processors who produce or handle products containing barley, rye, or oats. These companies would be required to clearly label products containing these ingredients and follow allergen control procedures to prevent cross-contamination. Consumers with allergies or sensitivities to these grains would benefit from clearer labeling and better information about product contents.  The practical impact would include mandatory allergen labeling for any food product containing these grains, similar to existing requirements for the current nine major allergens. This would help consumers with barley, rye, or oat allergies more easily identify and avoid products that could trigger allergic reactions."
