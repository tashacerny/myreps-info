summary: "# Bill Summary  This bill would eliminate asset limits in certain federally funded means-tested public assistance programs. Asset limits are financial thresholds that determine whether individuals or households qualify for benefits—applicants with savings, vehicles, or other assets exceeding specified amounts may be deemed ineligible, even if their income is low. By removing these limits, the bill would allow people with more accumulated assets to qualify for assistance programs.  The bill would affect individuals and families who use means-tested federal assistance programs, which typically include programs such as Supplemental Security Income (SSI), Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF), the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), and Medicaid. Currently, these programs use both income and asset tests to determine eligibility. The changes would apply to programs specified in the legislation, though the full text is needed to identify precisely which programs are included.  The primary effect of eliminating asset limits would be to broaden eligibility for these assistance programs. Proponents argue this would help people who have modest savings, vehicles, or homes but still need support. The bill could affect program costs and the number of beneficiaries, though the fiscal impact would depend on which programs are covered and how many additional people become eligible."
