summary: "# Bill Summary  This bill would amend federal health insurance regulations to cap cost sharing (copayments, coinsurance, and deductibles) that patients must pay for prescription drugs. Specifically, the legislation would prohibit patients' out-of-pocket costs from exceeding the nationwide average consumer purchase price for a given drug. The bill affects three major federal statutes: the Public Health Service Act (which governs health insurance), the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (which covers employer-sponsored plans), and the Internal Revenue Code.  The proposal would apply to health insurance plans regulated under these three laws, meaning it could affect most private health insurance coverage in the United States, including employer-sponsored plans and certain other insurance products. The bill targets situations where insurance plans charge patients amounts that exceed what the average uninsured consumer would pay for the same medication.  The key provision establishes a cost-sharing limit tied to nationwide average consumer purchase prices, preventing insurance plans from requiring patients to pay more than this benchmark amount out of pocket. This would likely affect how insurance plans structure their drug formularies, pricing tiers, and patient cost-sharing obligations."
