summary: "# Summary of the Ending the Carried Interest Loophole Act  This bill would change how the federal government taxes carried interest—compensation that investment fund managers (such as those in private equity, hedge funds, and real estate funds) receive as a share of investment profits. Currently, carried interest is often taxed as capital gains at lower rates (15–20%) rather than as ordinary income (up to 37%). The bill would require carried interest to be taxed as ordinary income for federal tax purposes.  **Who it affects:** The legislation would directly impact investment fund managers and partners who receive carried interest as compensation. It would also affect the funds they manage and potentially their investors. Individual taxpayers may be affected indirectly if provisions change investment returns or fund structures.  **Key provisions:** The bill reclassifies carried interest as ordinary income rather than capital gains, which would increase the tax rate applied to this compensation. This change would apply to investment managers across various fund types. The reclassification would likely increase federal tax revenue from this income source, though the exact amount depends on implementation details and how the investment industry may adjust its practices in response."
