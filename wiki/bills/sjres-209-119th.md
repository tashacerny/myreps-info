summary: "# Summary of Congressional Disapproval Resolution  This joint resolution seeks to use the Congressional Review Act (CRA) to disapprove a specific Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) rule regarding California's nonroad engine pollution control standards for ocean-going vessels while at berth (docked). Under the CRA, Congress can disapprove federal agency regulations within 60 legislative days of their submission. If passed by both chambers and signed by the President (or passed over a presidential veto), this resolution would nullify the EPA rule and prevent the agency from issuing substantially similar rules without new congressional authorization.  The rule in question relates to California's authority to set its own vehicle and engine emission standards—a power California has held under Section 209 of the Clean Air Act. The specific provision addresses pollution from ocean-going vessels when they are docked and connected to shore-based power or equipment rather than running their own engines. This affects shipping ports, vessel operators, and parties involved in maritime commerce, as well as state and federal environmental regulators.  The key issue before Congress is whether to overturn the EPA's decision regarding this California pollution control standard. Passage would eliminate the rule, while defeat would allow it to remain in effect. The resolution does not propose an alternative environmental policy but rather is a straightforward approve-or-disapprove mechanism on the existing EPA rule."
