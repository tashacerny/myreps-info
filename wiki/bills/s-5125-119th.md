summary: "# Bill Summary: Visitability Standards for Federally Assisted Housing  This bill would require newly constructed single-family houses and townhouses that receive federal financial assistance to meet minimum visitability standards designed for people with disabilities. Visitability refers to basic accessibility features that allow visitors and residents with mobility limitations—such as those using wheelchairs, walkers, or canes—to navigate and use homes more easily. The bill applies only to new construction projects that receive federal funding or support.  The bill would establish minimum design standards that likely include features such as no-step or zero-step entries, wider doorways and hallways to accommodate wheelchairs, and at least one accessible bathroom on the main floor. These standards would be required for all newly built properties receiving federal assistance, affecting developers, builders, and property owners who participate in federally funded housing programs.  The measure aims to increase the supply of accessible housing stock without requiring extensive or costly retrofits to existing homes, as the standards would apply only to new construction. Affected parties would include federal housing program participants, builders and developers, and people with disabilities seeking accessible housing options."
