summary: "# Bill Summary  This bill would amend federal health insurance laws to require no-cost coverage of annual mammography screening for women beginning at age 30. Currently, federal guidelines generally recommend routine mammography screening to begin at age 40 or 50, depending on risk factors and individual circumstances. This bill would lower the age at which insurers must cover screening mammograms without patient cost-sharing (such as copayments or deductibles).  The bill affects three major federal health programs: Medicare (Title XVIII), Medicaid (Title XIX), and private health insurance plans regulated under the Public Health Service Act (Title XXVII). This means the requirement would apply to coverage under Medicare, state Medicaid programs, and health plans sold in the private market. Affected populations include women age 30 and older who are enrolled in these programs or plans.  Key provisions would eliminate cost barriers to mammography screening for this broader age group by requiring insurers to cover annual screening mammograms with no out-of-pocket expenses. The bill does not appear to address other aspects of mammography policy, such as screening frequency recommendations, diagnostic procedures, or coverage for women under age 30."
