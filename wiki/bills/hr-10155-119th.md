summary: "# Bill Summary  This bill proposes to amend federal law governing military retirement to allow certain reserve component members to count prior active duty service toward reducing their eligibility age for retirement. Currently, reserve members generally must reach age 60 to retire with benefits. The bill would enable those with qualifying active duty experience to become eligible for retirement benefits at a younger age by crediting that prior service.  The change would primarily affect members of the Reserve and National Guard who previously served on active duty. It would allow their earlier active duty service to count toward the reduced retirement eligibility age, making retirement benefits accessible sooner than under current rules. This could benefit career military personnel who transition between active and reserve components during their service.  The bill addresses a timing issue in military compensation and retention: it recognizes that service members with both active and reserve experience have contributed longer to the military overall, even if their reserve service began later in their careers. The specific eligibility thresholds and implementation details would depend on the bill's full text."
