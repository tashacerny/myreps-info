summary: "# Summary: Prediction Markets Are Gambling Act  **What the Bill Proposes**  Based on the title, this bill would classify prediction markets as gambling under federal law. Prediction markets are platforms where participants buy and sell contracts that pay out based on the occurrence of future events (such as election results, economic indicators, or other outcomes). The bill would treat these markets similarly to other gambling activities rather than as financial instruments or information markets.  **Who It Affects**  This legislation would impact prediction market operators and platforms, their users, and potentially regulators. It could affect companies that currently operate prediction markets, individuals who participate in them, and may alter how federal agencies like the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) or the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) oversee these platforms. Depending on implementation, it could also affect researchers and policymakers who use prediction markets to gather data on public expectations.  **Key Implications**  If enacted, the bill would likely impose gambling regulations on prediction markets, which could include licensing requirements, restrictions on participation, or outright prohibitions—depending on how broadly the law is written and enforced. This represents a shift from the current regulatory landscape, where some prediction markets operate under limited CFTC exemptions. The change would fundamentally alter the legal status and operational framework for these platforms."
