summary: "# Bill Summary  This bill proposes to amend the Terrorism Risk Insurance Act of 2002 (TRIA), which created a federal program to help the insurance industry manage risks from terrorist attacks. Specifically, the bill seeks to clarify the timing and procedures for determining whether an entity qualifies as an \"agency or instrumentality of a terrorist party.\" This determination is important because it affects insurance coverage eligibility and liability protections under TRIA.  The bill would establish clearer rules about *when* an entity's status as a terrorist-related agency or instrumentality should be assessed. Currently, ambiguity exists regarding whether this determination should occur at the time of an insurance claim, at the time a policy is issued, or at some other point in the process. By clarifying this timing, the bill aims to reduce disputes between insurers, policyholders, and the federal government over coverage decisions.  The bill affects insurance companies, their customers, and federal agencies responsible for administering the TRIA program. The change is primarily procedural and technical rather than substantive—it does not alter which organizations are designated as terrorist parties, but rather clarifies *when* that designation status should be evaluated for insurance purposes. This is intended to provide greater certainty and consistency in how terrorism-related insurance claims are handled."
