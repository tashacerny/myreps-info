summary: "# Bill Summary  This bill would amend Part B of Title IV of the Social Security Act, which governs child welfare and protective services. The primary purpose is to establish a requirement that child protective services (CPS) systems cannot separate children from their parents based solely on poverty or economic hardship. The bill aims to clarify that economic conditions alone—such as inability to afford housing, food, or utilities—should not be grounds for removing a child from parental custody.  The bill would affect state child protective services agencies, caseworkers, and families involved in the child welfare system. It may also impact federal oversight of state CPS practices, as states typically receive federal funding for child welfare programs under Title IV. By restricting CPS authority to separate children on poverty grounds alone, the bill seeks to distinguish between poverty and neglect or abuse, recognizing that economic disadvantage does not necessarily indicate a parent is harming or endangering a child.  Key provisions likely include a prohibition on separation decisions made solely due to economic factors, possible requirements that CPS agencies consider alternative interventions (such as connecting families with social services and support programs), and potentially provisions for training or guidance to ensure state agencies comply with this standard. The bill may also include mechanisms for federal monitoring or enforcement of these requirements."
