summary: "# Legislative Summary: Forced Arbitration Prohibition Bill  This bill would prohibit employers from requiring employees to use arbitration as a mandatory means of resolving workplace disputes. Currently, many employment contracts include clauses that require workers to settle disagreements—such as those involving wages, discrimination, harassment, or safety violations—through private arbitration rather than through the court system or regulatory agencies. The bill would make such mandatory arbitration agreements unenforceable in employment relationships, allowing workers to pursue claims through courts or other legal channels if they choose.  The bill would affect employers and employees across industries, particularly those in sectors where arbitration clauses are common. It would also impact arbitration firms and legal processes, as workplace disputes would potentially shift from private arbitration back to public court systems and government agencies like the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC).  Key provisions would likely include making pre-employment arbitration agreements void and unenforceable, protecting employees' right to pursue legal claims individually or collectively (including class actions), and ensuring access to courts and administrative agencies for workplace disputes. The bill may also address existing arbitration agreements and include exceptions or transition provisions."
