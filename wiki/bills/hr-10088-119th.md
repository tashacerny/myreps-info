summary: "# Bill Summary  This bill directs the Secretary of Health and Human Services (HHS), through the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), to conduct a study on reprocessed single-use medical devices. Single-use devices are medical instruments designed and approved by the FDA for one-time use; reprocessing refers to cleaning, sterilizing, and reusing these devices. The study would assess whether expanding the reprocessing of such devices is safe and effective.  The bill affects healthcare providers, hospitals, medical device manufacturers, and potentially patients who receive care using medical devices. It also involves the FDA, which currently regulates reprocessed devices. The study would examine the feasibility and safety of expanding reprocessing practices beyond current applications, likely considering factors such as device types suitable for reprocessing, sterilization methods, quality standards, and regulatory frameworks needed.  The practical impact of this legislation depends on the study's findings and any subsequent FDA actions. If reprocessing is found to be safe and effective for additional device types, it could reduce healthcare costs by extending device utility, though it could also affect device manufacturers' sales. The study itself does not authorize or mandate reprocessing but rather gathers information to inform future policy decisions."
