summary: "# Summary: Concurrent Care for Comfort Act  **What the Bill Proposes**  The Concurrent Care for Comfort Act addresses Medicare policy regarding palliative care and hospice services. Specifically, it allows Medicare beneficiaries to receive hospice care while simultaneously pursuing curative or life-prolonging medical treatments—rather than requiring them to choose one or the other. Under current Medicare rules, patients must typically elect hospice care and forgo curative treatments, or continue curative treatment and remain ineligible for hospice benefits. This bill would remove that restriction.  **Who It Affects**  The primary beneficiaries are Medicare-eligible patients with serious or terminal illnesses who wish to receive comfort-focused care (such as pain management and emotional support) while also continuing treatments aimed at extending life or curing their condition. The policy change also affects hospice providers, oncologists, and other physicians involved in treating these patients, as it expands the circumstances under which hospice services can be billed to Medicare.  **Key Provisions**  The bill permits concurrent receipt of hospice and curative care services under Medicare. This allows patients and their doctors greater flexibility in tailoring care to individual circumstances and preferences, rather than forcing a binary choice. The change applies specifically to Medicare coverage policy and does not mandate concurrent care—it simply makes it an option for those who elect it."
